Bonjour Paris 🗼

Paris felt like stepping into a long-awaited dream: cobblestone streets, warm café windows, and the Seine glimmering under evening lights.

By AdminCity of LoveFrance

I had wanted Paris for years before I finally went. Not in a vague “bucket list” way — more like that quiet itch that shows up whenever someone mentions the Seine or a metro map flashes on screen. By the time the plane started descending and the Eiffel Tower actually appeared outside the window, it felt a little absurd how emotional that first glimpse was.

On the ground, the city is smaller and messier than the postcards. Cobblestones, café chairs half-blocking the pavement, bakery smells that make you stop mid-walk. The Seine in daylight is bright and a bit ordinary; at night it turns into something else entirely.

Where we stayed

We booked Grand Hôtel Lévêque for 2 nights / 3 days. For a first Paris trip, the location did most of the heavy lifting.

  • Almost in the center — easy to walk out and start exploring without a big commute.
  • Airport bus stop nearby, which saved us one transfer headache on arrival.
  • Eiffel Tower is walkable from here; so is a metro station.
  • Plenty of cafés and small restaurants around, so you can eat without planning every meal.
  • Rooms are small. We mostly slept there, so it was fine — neat and clean, nothing fancy.

If you are the kind of traveler who wants a big lobby and a spa, this is not that hotel. If you want to wake up, walk out, and be in Paris within two minutes, it works.

Dinner cruise on the Seine

One evening we booked Paris en Scène Diner Croisière. The boat loops past the Eiffel Tower stretch of the river, and once the lights come on, you stop caring much about the food plating and just watch the city slide by.

You can usually find tickets on GetYourGuide or Viator. Meal (veg / non-veg options) and a welcome drink are included; extra drinks are charged separately. Go for the night sailing if you can — the tower lit up from the water is the part that sticks.

Small things that made the days feel real

We did not try to “finish” Paris. A few hours by the river, a long walk with no destination, espresso on a terrace while people argued politely about nothing important. That was enough. The famous sights matter, but so does the stretch of street between them — the one you only notice because you got slightly lost.

If you are coming for the first time: keep one evening free of tickets and itineraries. Let the city decide the route.
